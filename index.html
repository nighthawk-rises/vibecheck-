How the scores workWhat goes into each score, and the sources!

Every market gets a score out of 100 and a rating:

Bitcoin uses the official alternative.me Fear & Greed index as is.

Solana and the stocks use fixed lines: RSI, place in the one-year range, and distance from the 200-day average. SpaceX uses its range and average since the June IPO; it has too little history to test.

Stock market Fear & Greed is CNN's index for the whole US market, shown as is. It does not feed the other scores.

The collectible scores share one recipe, built from each item's own past sale prices: the typical item's gap to its own 12-month average (about half the weight), the typical move over the past month (about a third) and the share of items rising (the rest). It reads how hot each market runs against its own past. The premium over launch price still shows for boxes but is not scored.

N64 & GameCube covers 136 games, loose and boxed: PriceCharting's most popular on each console, minus hardware and variant editions. Their prices come from PriceCharting's console price lists, one page per console. Single game prices snap back, the clearest pattern in the tests, so the game picks lean on it.

Pokémon sealed covers 40 boxes: every English main-set booster box since 2020, plus each special set's Elite Trainer Box. Hot boxes kept rising in the tests, so read this score as the market's mood, not a signal to time.

Sales a day is PriceCharting's own count, shown but not scored.

Best buys and sells come in two groups. Markets: crypto and stocks, only at the extremes, Very low or Very high, judged after 30 days. Collectibles: up to three games a side, far under or over their usual price, judged after 3 months with a second look at 6, since games sell slowly and eBay's fee of about 13% plus shipping eats a quick flip.

Connecting dots are patterns Claude finds across the page's data and history, checked against the numbers. Moon shots are the ten most undervalued assets it has found outside this page, where a win could pay several times what a loss would cost, ranked by how sure it is. A pick keeps its place until news changes its case; every Monday Claude searches for stronger ones. Neither is scored. Each moon shot is checked against the S&P 500 over the same days: while listed, and at fixed checks 30 and 90 days after it was spotted. Are the picks working? compares each group's hit rate with luck: how often each asset moved the call’s way over the same length of time, 30 days or 3 months, in the three years before.

Since yesterday compares with the previous morning's run. 10k Relay and Are the picks working? keep every day, so the long ranges grow each morning.

Sources: alternative.me, CNN, CoinGecko, Public.com, Stock Analysis and Google Finance (stocks, VOO and the metal funds; Yahoo Finance when it answers), and PriceCharting (console price lists for the games, item pages for the Pokémon boxes). Every stock and fund price is checked against its date, since the fetch tool sometimes returns old copies of a page. Past prices come from Yahoo Finance and PriceCharting. A scheduled Claude run rebuilds the page each morning, and a second one checks prices four times a day for very big moves.
